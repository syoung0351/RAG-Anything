# Engineering Leadership Frameworks for Project Managers

## IC-to-Manager Transition

The most common failure mode when an individual contributor moves into engineering management is continuing to solve problems with code instead of through people. Key reframe: your output is now team velocity and morale, not commits.

**First 90 days checklist:**
- Schedule weekly 1:1s with every direct report within week 1
- Shadow two customer calls or user research sessions
- Read every open P0/P1 bug and understand why it wasn’t prioritized
- Identify the informal influencer on the team — they are often more effective than the hierarchy

## 1:1 Structure

A 1:1 is not a status update. If the conversation is about tickets, you are doing it wrong.

Effective 1:1 agenda:
1. **How are you doing?** (2 min) — emotional state, not project state
2. **What’s blocking you?** (5 min) — obstacles the manager can remove
3. **Career topic** (10 min) — rotating focus: growth goals, feedback, skills gap
4. **Anything else?** (3 min) — their agenda, not yours

Keep a shared doc. Both parties add topics. The engineer drives the agenda.

## Performance Framework

The two axes that matter: **impact** (what got shipped, what changed) and **influence** (how the team around them improved).

Avoid rating engineers solely on story points or tickets closed. Measure:
- Did the codebase get easier to work in after they touched it?
- Did their design reviews raise the quality of others’ work?
- Did junior engineers grow faster because of their mentorship?

Performance conversations should never be a surprise. Feedback belongs in the 1:1, not the annual review.

## Hiring Bar

Define the bar *before* you open the role. Answer three questions:
1. What specific problem does this person solve in the next 12 months?
2. What does “exceptional” look like for this role? What does “acceptable” look like?
3. What seniority level is this, and what is non-negotiable vs. trainable?

In interviews, evaluate evidence, not confidence. “Tell me about a time when...” beats “How would you design...”

Common hiring mistakes:
- Hiring for interview performance, not job performance
- Lowering the bar when the team is under pressure
- Ignoring culture-add for culture-fit (the latter creates monocultures)

## Psychological Safety

Amy Edmondson’s research is clear: teams with psychological safety ship faster and recover from failure better.

Tactical practices:
- When something breaks, ask “what did the system allow?” before “who did this?”
- In retros, the manager shares their own mistake first
- Publicly acknowledge when you changed your mind based on an engineer’s input
- Never penalize a “dumb question” in a meeting

## Glue Work Recognition

Glue work — documentation, onboarding help, cross-team coordination, test infrastructure improvement — is disproportionately done by underrepresented engineers and disproportionately unrewarded.

As a PM or manager, explicitly reward glue work in performance reviews. Quantify it: “reduced onboarding time from 3 weeks to 1 week” is measurable impact.

## Tech Lead vs. Engineering Manager

| Tech Lead | Engineering Manager |
|---|---|
| Defines technical direction | Defines career direction |
| Reviews code and architecture | Reviews performance and growth |
| Answers “how do we build this?” | Answers “who builds this and how do they grow?” |
| Individual contributor + multiplier | Manager of people |
| Accountable for technical quality | Accountable for team health and delivery |

Some organizations combine both into a “tech lead manager” role. This is a high-risk arrangement — the dual accountability often means neither gets full attention.
