# Stakeholder Strategy and Management

## Power/Interest Grid

Map every key stakeholder on two axes:
- **Power:** their ability to affect your project’s success or failure
- **Interest:** how much they care about the project’s outcome

```
High Power │  Manage closely   │  Keep satisfied
           │  (engage, involve  │  (meet their needs,
           │  in key decisions) │  don't overburden)
──────────┼───────────────────┼────────────────
Low Power  │  Monitor          │  Keep informed
           │  (minimal effort) │  (regular updates)
           └───────────────────┴────────────────
              Low Interest         High Interest
```

## Influence Without Authority

**Influence levers:**
1. **Expertise:** Be the most credible person in the room on your domain.
2. **Reciprocity:** Give before you ask. Help stakeholders with their priorities.
3. **Coalition building:** Win the coalition, not individual battles.
4. **Framing:** Present decisions in terms of the stakeholder’s priorities, not yours.
5. **Scarcity:** Surface the real cost of delay.

## Converting Resistant Stakeholders

| Root Cause | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Information gap | “I don’t understand why we’re doing this” | Explain the problem and decision criteria |
| Values conflict | “This goes against how we operate” | Find common ground on the underlying value |
| Loss of control | “This affects my team without my involvement” | Involve them earlier; give a decision role |
| Past experience | “We tried this before and it failed” | Acknowledge history; explain what’s different |

Avoid arguing with resistant stakeholders in a group setting. Address resistance 1:1.

## Executive Communication

**The pyramid principle:** Lead with the recommendation. Follow with 3 supporting points. Details only if asked.

**What executives actually want to know:**
1. What decision do you need from me?
2. What is the cost of inaction?
3. What risk am I taking on?
4. Have you thought through second-order effects?

## RACI Matrix

- **Responsible:** does the work
- **Accountable:** owns the outcome (one person only)
- **Consulted:** input sought before the decision
- **Informed:** notified after the decision

Common mistakes: multiple accountable owners (no one is accountable), or treating “consulted” as “must approve” (creates bottlenecks).
